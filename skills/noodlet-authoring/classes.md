# Classes, students, and progress

## First: which shape of class is this?

Every class has a name, a set of assigned noodlets, and a reusable **link** learners
follow. What that link does when someone opens it comes in two shapes, and most of this
page depends on which:

| Shape | Following the link gets you | Teacher sees |
|---|---|---|
| **Keeps a register** | An enrolment — a named person the class remembers between lessons | Who did what, across a term |
| **Keeps no register** | A numbered seat — anonymous, and gone at the end of the day | A live board of seats, cleared after 24 hours |

**Find out which before you plan anything around a roster.** Ask the teacher, or look at
the class in the teacher app — the classes page says which kind each one is, and the
"New class" step says which kinds this account can create. One teacher can hold both
shapes at once, so it is a fact about the class, not about them.

Classes that keep a register store information about named children, so they are enabled
after a conversation with the Noodlet team rather than self-serve. Classes that keep none
work straight away, for everyone, everywhere, with nothing to sign — if you're not sure
which the teacher has, that is the one to assume.

## Create a class

Teacher app → **Classes** → **New class**. Each class gets a reusable link — share it by
email, chat, or QR code. Rotate it in class settings if it leaks; people already enrolled
are unaffected.

## Classes that keep a register

Students who follow the link join immediately: logged-in accounts enrol under their
account; others join anonymously (device-bound, and progress is lost if they clear
cookies or switch devices unless they claim an account).

### Enrol students — three ways

1. **Invite link (self-service)** — share the class URL; students join on their own. Best
   where students already have (or will create) their own accounts.
2. **Add student** — for students with no Noodlet account yet. Provide a username
   (globally unique) and optional display name; an initial password is shown for the
   teacher to pass on.
3. **Invite by username** — for students who already have an account. They see an
   accept/reject prompt the next time they log in.

Anonymous students can register or log in at any time; Noodlet automatically claims their
anonymous progress onto the account — no teacher action needed. If they already had an
account-bound enrolment in the same class, the two merge, uniting all attempts under a
single student identity.

### Track progress

Class → **Progress** — per student: attempt count, best score (0–1 mastery fraction), and
whether they completed the lesson.

### Evaluation classes

Some accounts have **evaluation** classes, which are the same thing with a shorter life:
for consenting adult testers rather than pupils, capped at 35 people, closing to new
activity at 21 days and deleted at 90. Everything above applies unchanged; an evaluation
class can't be converted to a permanent one, so when the school signs, they create a new
one.

## Classes that keep no register

The class is a name, its noodlets and a link. Whoever opens the link gets a numbered seat
and starts straight away — no account, no sign-in, nothing stored about who they are. It
works with no teacher present, so setting it as homework is an ordinary use.

- **Nobody is enrolled**, so there is no roster, no adding, and no inviting.
- **There is no per-student progress view.** Attempts are recorded against a seat number.
- The teacher watches it live at Class → the class's board, which shows seats working in
  real time and clears after 24 hours. Each seat gets a bar per activity: empty until they
  open it, filling as they work, green when they finish. They can write a name against a
  seat to make the board readable and export it — both stay in their own browser and are
  never sent to Noodlet.
- If the teacher needs to know how a particular child is getting on over time, that needs
  a class that keeps a register: hello@noodlet.com.

## Assign a noodlet (both shapes)

Class → **Assignments** → add the noodlet. Everyone on the class sees it in their lesson
list immediately. Assignments survive an update — the noodlet's URL stays stable when
you rebuild it with new files. A noodlet can be assigned to several classes.
